How to read an entry

Every sensor below has the same five rows, because those are the five things you need to know before you can either build its front-end or write its firmware:

  • Measures — the physical quantity and its useful range.
  • Signal — the electrical form it arrives in, its ECU pins, and its wire class from the Kirloskar diagram's own colour legend.
  • Acquisition — the front-end circuit, which MCU peripheral reads it, and how often.
  • Used for — what the engine does with the number. This is the row that matters and the one most sensor documentation leaves out.
  • Fault detection — how firmware knows the reading is lying.

Sampling rates are proposals, not measurements — they follow from each signal's own bandwidth and from what the control loop using it needs. Marked inferred where that is the case.

A note on sample rate

The instinct is to sample everything fast. Resist it. Coolant temperature has a thermal time constant measured in tens of seconds, so sampling it at 1 kHz buys nothing and costs ADC bandwidth that rail pressure genuinely needs. Rate should follow the physics, and the physics here spans four orders of magnitude — from crank edges every 667 µs to a fuel level that moves over hours.

01

Position & speed

Everything angle-related derives from these two. Get them wrong and nothing else in the system can be right, because injection timing is meaningless without knowing where the crankshaft is.

Crankshaft speed

pins 52 / 74 / 30on ECU
Measures
Crank angular position and speed, from a toothed wheel. Nominal running speed 1500 rpm; must also resolve cranking speed, roughly 100–250 rpm.
Signal
Variable reluctance, differential. Frequency I/P High on pin 52, Frequency I/P Low on pin 74, sensor ground on pin 30. Twisted pair. Output is AC whose amplitude scales with speed — near-zero while cranking, several volts at idle.
Acquisition
Zero-cross adaptive-threshold conditioner producing one clean digital edge per tooth, into a FlexTimer input capture channel. Every edge is timestamped; at 1500 rpm with a 60-tooth wheel that is an edge every 667 µs. The missing-tooth gap gives the angular reference.
Used for
Speed governing — the primary feedback for the metering unit loop. Injection timing — every injection event is scheduled against crank angle derived from here. Overspeed shutdown — the ECU's own first line of defence. Crank sequencing — the starter is disengaged the moment speed crosses the catch threshold. Reported over CAN in EEC1, PGN 61444.
Fault detection
Missing edges, implausible tooth intervals, or loss of the sync gap. Loss of crank signal on a running engine is a shutdown, not an alarm — the ECU can no longer time injection.

Camshaft position

pins 45 / 46 / 44on ECU
Measures
Which half of the four-stroke cycle the crank angle refers to. One pulse per two crank revolutions.
Signal
Hall effect, already a clean digital square wave — which is why it needs no conditioning IC and has no Low pin. Switch Battery 5 V on pin 45, Frequency I/P on pin 46, sensor ground on pin 44.
Acquisition
Pull-up to the protected sensor rail, straight into a second FlexTimer capture channel. One event per 80 ms at running speed.
Used for
Cylinder phase, and only that. The crank sensor says the engine is at 15° before top dead centre; the cam sensor says whether that is cylinder 1's compression stroke or its exhaust stroke. Without it you can measure speed perfectly and still inject into the wrong cylinder.
Fault detection
Absent pulse, or a pulse at the wrong crank angle relative to the sync gap. Loss after sync is survivable for a while — the engine can run on the last known phase — but it must not be allowed to re-sync blind.
02

Fuel system

Rail pressure

pins 32 / 35 / 08on ECU
Measures
Common-rail fuel pressure. Typically 250–1800 bar on an engine of this class — inferred, the exact range needs the sensor part number.
Signal
Ratiometric 0.5–4.5 V. Switch Battery 5 V on pin 32, Analog I/P on pin 35, sensor ground on pin 08 — a dedicated ground, not spliced with any other sensor. That is deliberate: this is the most accuracy-critical analog channel on the engine, and a shared return would inject other sensors' currents into its reference.
Acquisition
Ratiometric front-end into the ADC. Sampled fast — 1 kHz or better, inferred — because it is the feedback for a closed loop that has to reject the pressure ripple every injection event causes.
Used for
Closed-loop rail pressure control: the error between measured and target pressure drives the fuel metering unit's PWM duty. Also sets the injection quantity calculation — the same pulse width delivers more fuel at higher rail pressure, so quantity is always a function of both. Over-pressure protection is a hard shutdown.
Fault detection
Out-of-range reading, or pressure that fails to follow a metering unit command — which distinguishes a dead sensor from a stuck metering unit. Loss of rail pressure feedback means injection quantity becomes unknown; treat as shutdown.

Fuel metering unit current

pin 88, sensed on-boardon ECU
Measures
Current through the metering unit solenoid — the actuator's own feedback, not an external sensor.
Signal
Shunt and amplifier on the ECU's low-side driver. The metering unit itself is only 2-pin: battery via the 20 A fuse, and PWM low side to pin 88.
Acquisition
ADC channel synchronised to the PWM cycle, so current is sampled at a consistent point in the switching period rather than at random phase.
Used for
Inner loop of the rail pressure cascade. Rail pressure sets a target current; current control sets PWM duty. Two loops because solenoid force follows current, and current follows duty only loosely once coil temperature and battery voltage move. Also gives open and short circuit detection on the actuator.
Fault detection
Zero current with duty commanded means an open coil or blown fuse. Full current at zero duty means a shorted driver — which is dangerous, since it drives the rail to maximum pressure.

Injector current, ×3

pins 73 / 07 / 29, sensed on-boardon ECU
Measures
Current through each injector solenoid during its firing event. Peak 12–24 A, hold 8–13 A.
Signal
Sense resistor in each low-side leg, read by the driver IC's own current comparators.
Acquisition
The MC33816 regulates current in hardware against programmed thresholds — the MCU sets the profile, the driver enforces it microsecond by microsecond. Far too fast for firmware to close.
Used for
Peak-and-hold profile enforcement. A high current opens the needle, then a lower current holds it, because holding at peak would cook the solenoid. Also injector diagnosis: a coil that fails open or short shows up immediately in the current trace.
Fault detection
Failure to reach peak within the expected time means an open coil or a collapsed boost rail. The handbook's injector chart shows why that timing is diagnostic — the rise time is set by the rail voltage and the coil, so a slow rise means one of them is wrong.
03

Air path

Boost pressure

pins 11 / 41 / 34on ECU
Measures
Absolute pressure in the intake manifold, downstream of the turbocharger and aftercooler. Typically 1–3 bar absolute on a turbocharged engine of this size — inferred.
Signal
Ratiometric 0.5–4.5 V. One half of a four-pin combination sensor shared with manifold air temperature: Switch Battery 5 V on pin 11, Analog I/P Pressure on pin 41, sensor ground on pin 34 shared with both legs and with the coolant sensor.
Acquisition
Ratiometric front-end into the ADC. 100 Hz is ample — manifold pressure changes with turbo speed, which has real inertia. Inferred.
Used for
Air mass estimation, with air temperature, via the ideal gas law — and air mass is what sets the smoke-limit fuelling cap. A diesel that injects more fuel than its air charge can burn produces visible smoke and heat rather than torque, so the fuelling limit tracks boost. Also EGR control, since EGR displaces fresh air, and turbo overspeed protection.
Fault detection
Range check, plus plausibility against barometric pressure at rest — with the engine stopped, manifold pressure must read close to ambient. A sensor that fails high would silently allow over-fuelling, so this one defaults conservative on fault.

Manifold air temperature

pin 79topology open — U2
Measures
Intake charge temperature after the aftercooler. Roughly −20 to +100 °C.
Signal
Analog I/P Temperature on pin 79, sharing the combination sensor's supply and ground. Whether it is an NTC or a conditioned ratiometric output is unresolved. The wire colour class cannot distinguish them — the coolant NTC uses the same magenta class. Industry convention for combination pressure-and-temperature sensors is an NTC element, so that is the working assumption.
Acquisition
Pull-up divider if NTC, ratiometric front-end if not. The board carries a DNP pull-up footprint so either works without a respin — the cheapest possible insurance against a question we cannot currently answer.
Used for
Air density correction. Boost pressure alone does not give air mass; the same pressure at 20 °C and at 80 °C is meaningfully different charge. Feeds the same smoke-limit calculation. Also intercooler diagnostics — a rising delta over ambient means the aftercooler is fouling.
Fault detection
Range check, and plausibility against coolant temperature at cold start: on a machine that has stood overnight, intake air, coolant and ambient should all agree within a few degrees. That check is free and catches a surprising number of wiring errors.

EGR valve position

pins 15 / 37 / 36on ECU
Measures
Actual position of the EGR valve, 0–100% travel.
Signal
Ratiometric potentiometer. Switch Battery 5 V on pin 15, Analog I/P wiper on pin 37, sensor ground on pin 36. Shares its five-pin connector with the actuator's own EGR High and EGR Low drive pins.
Acquisition
Ratiometric front-end into the ADC, 100 Hz, matched to the valve's mechanical response. Inferred.
Used for
Closed-loop EGR position control — the H-bridge drives until measured position matches demand. EGR rate is one of the two things this engine uses to meet its NOx limit, the other being injection timing, which is exactly why the certification question in the handbook bites so hard. Also stuck-valve detection.
Fault detection
Position failing to follow demand within a timeout means a stuck valve, a failed motor, or a broken position sensor — distinguishable by whether current is flowing. A stuck-open EGR valve at load is a derate condition.

Intake throttle position

6-pin connector, routing unknownunresolved — U8
Measures
Intake throttle plate position. On a CPCB IV+ diesel the throttle is not a power control — it creates intake depression to drive EGR flow when the pressure differential is otherwise insufficient.
Signal
Six pins, which is two more than a motor pair plus a three-wire position sensor accounts for. The extra two are unexplained. It does not appear on any ECU pin traced in the wiring diagram — it may belong to a variant, or route somewhere we have not found.
Acquisition
Not designed. Blocked until the connector is traced on the engine.
Used for
If present: EGR flow authority at low load, and a clean shutdown — closing the throttle starves the engine of air and stops it smoothly.
Fault detection
Not designed.
04

Engine health

These are the sensors that stop the engine before it destroys itself. They earn their place in an ECU that never optimises anything.

Coolant temperature

pin 33on ECU
Measures
Engine coolant temperature, −40 to +130 °C. Normal running is roughly 80–95 °C.
Signal
NTC thermistor, two wires only — no excitation pin, unlike the ratiometric sensors. Analog I/P on pin 33; its sensor ground does not get its own ECU pin but splices into the boost sensor's return at pin 34.
Acquisition
Pull-up divider into the ADC, linearised in firmware against the sensor's resistance-temperature table. 2 Hz is plenty — an engine block's thermal time constant is tens of seconds. The pull-up is chosen to put maximum sensitivity around 85 °C, where the decisions are.
Used for
Cold-start fuelling enrichment — a cold diesel needs more fuel and earlier injection to light off. Overheat derate then shutdown. EGR inhibit when cold, since EGR on a cold engine fouls the intake. Reported over CAN in ET1, PGN 65262.
Fault detection
Open circuit reads full pull-up voltage and short reads zero — both outside any physical temperature, so both are unambiguous. Critically, an open circuit must not read as "very cold", which would trigger enrichment on a hot engine. The independent coolant switch below exists precisely because this channel can fail.

Coolant temperature switch

pin 43, return on 01 / 02on ECU
Measures
A single over-temperature threshold. Not a measurement — a trip.
Signal
Digital I/P on pin 43, switch-to-return. Its return leg is one of the G_G_B pins.
Acquisition
Pull-up and RC debounce into a GPIO, polled at 10 ms.
Used for
A second, independent path to overheat shutdown. This is the same design instinct as the overspeed discussion in the handbook: the analog coolant channel and this switch fail in different ways, so requiring only one of them to trip means a single sensor failure cannot leave the engine cooking. It is cheap redundancy on the failure that quietly destroys engines.
Fault detection
Wired normally-closed where possible, so an open harness reads as tripped rather than as healthy.

Lubricating oil pressure

pins 39 / 80on ECU
Measures
Engine oil gallery pressure, typically 0–10 bar. Normal running above roughly 2 bar; idle above roughly 1 bar. Inferred.
Signal
Ratiometric 0.5–4.5 V, three pins: Switch Battery 5 V on pin 39, Analog I/P on pin 80, and a sensor ground that splices with the EGR sensor's into pin 36.
Acquisition
Ratiometric front-end into the ADC at 10 Hz. Inferred — oil pressure responds within a revolution, but no decision needs faster.
Used for
Low oil pressure shutdown, the single most important protection on any engine — bearing damage from oil starvation happens in seconds. The threshold is speed-dependent: pressure that is normal at idle is a fault at rated speed, so the limit is a function of the crank sensor's reading. Also start inhibit until pressure builds, and reported over CAN in EFL/P1, PGN 65263.
Fault detection
Range check. The dangerous failure is reading high when pressure is actually low, so a plausibility check against engine state helps: with the engine stopped, oil pressure must read near zero. A sensor that reads 3 bar on a stopped engine is lying.
05

Electrical

Battery voltage, ×2

pins 04 / 06on ECU
Measures
Voltage on two separately fused battery rails, 6–40 V covering cranking sag through clamped load dump. Nominal 12 V, charging 13.5–14.4 V.
Signal
Two DC positive feeds, V_V_BAT_1R and V_V_BAT_2R, each behind its own 10 A fuse.
Acquisition
120 kΩ / 10 kΩ divider into the ADC — no active conditioning, this is a slow rail rather than a delicate signal. 10 Hz. The divider value was corrected from an inherited 75 kΩ / 10 kΩ that would have put 4.69 V on a 3.3 V pin.
Used for
Low and high battery alarms. Charging system diagnosis — voltage that never rises above resting means the alternator is not charging. Injector timing compensation, since boost converter recharge time depends on supply voltage. And because the two rails are separately fused, comparing them infers a blown fuse without a dedicated fuse-status circuit. Reported over CAN in VEP1, PGN 65271.
Fault detection
The two channels check each other: they should track within a volt. A persistent divergence means a fuse, a corroded connection, or a failed divider.

Ignition / run request

pin 71on ECU
Measures
Whether the panel is commanding the engine to run.
Signal
Switched battery, active high. Downstream of the GCU's 70 A ignition relay.
Acquisition
Zener-clamped bias network into a GPIO — not a divider, for the reason given in the handbook. Polled at 10 ms.
Used for
Run enable and orderly shutdown. Losing this input is the normal stop request: the ECU stops injection, closes the throttle if fitted, and reports the stop over CAN. It is also, per the handbook's safety section, the signalling half of a path whose other half is physical — the same relay that drops this input is believed to remove power from the fuel system entirely, which is what makes a hung ECU survivable.
Fault detection
Not detectable as an open circuit — an open wire and a genuine stop request are electrically identical. That is an accepted limitation of matching the OEM harness, and it is recorded in the FMEA rather than engineered around, because changing the sense would break compatibility.

Not sensors in the strict sense, but they arrive on the same connector and firmware treats them identically — debounced digital state with a defined meaning.

The override switch's actual function is a guess. Read its state, log it, report it over CAN — but do not let it change what the engine does until somebody has confirmed what the panel intends by it. A switch whose meaning you have assumed is worse than a switch you ignore.

What we would add

The OEM sensor set is the minimum to run the engine and meet emissions. These are the additions worth designing space for now, since a connector position or an unused ADC channel costs almost nothing at design time and cannot be retrofitted cheaply.

What future-proofing actually means here

Not fitting every sensor on the list. It means reserving the channels and connector positions so that adding one later is a firmware change and a harness leg, not a board respin. Concretely: leave four ADC channels unrouted but broken out, keep two spare positions on the sensor connector, put the I²C bus on a header even if only the barometric sensor uses it now, and leave the second CAN channel populated.

The MCU has room for all of it — the channel budget below shows the design using well under half of what the part provides. Spending some of that headroom on unpopulated options is the cheapest decision available at this stage.
