How to read an entry

Every sensor below has the same five rows, because those are the five things you need to know before you can either build its front-end or write its firmware:

  • Measures — the physical quantity and its useful range.
  • Signal — the electrical form it arrives in, its ECU pins, and its wire class from the Kirloskar diagram's own colour legend.
  • Acquisition — the front-end circuit, which MCU peripheral reads it, and how often.
  • Used for — what the engine does with the number. This is the row that matters and the one most sensor documentation leaves out.
  • Fault detection — how firmware knows the reading is lying.

Sampling rates are proposals, not measurements — they follow from each signal's own bandwidth and from what the control loop using it needs. Marked inferred where that is the case.

A note on sample rate

The instinct is to sample everything fast. Resist it. Coolant temperature has a thermal time constant measured in tens of seconds, so sampling it at 1 kHz buys nothing and costs ADC bandwidth that rail pressure genuinely needs. Rate should follow the physics, and the physics here spans four orders of magnitude — from crank edges every 667 µs to a fuel level that moves over hours.

01

Position & speed

Everything angle-related derives from these two. Get them wrong and nothing else in the system can be right, because injection timing is meaningless without knowing where the crankshaft is.

Crankshaft speed

pins 52 / 74 / 30on ECU
Part
Bosch DG-6-K, 0 281 002 214 (owner, 22 Nov 2026). Catalogue: 860 Ω, 370 mH; about 170 mV at 50 rpm and a 1.5 mm gap, up to 210 V at 7000 rpm and 0.3 mm (decisions log §28).
Measures
Crank angular position and speed, from a 60−2 toothed wheel. Nominal running speed 1500 rpm; must also resolve cranking speed, roughly 100–250 rpm.
Signal
Variable reluctance, differential, twisted. Bosch: sensor pin 1 to I_F_CRSNEG (pin 74), pin 2 to I_F_CRSPOS (pin 52), pin 3 to G_R_CRS (pin 30, shield / return). The amplitude scales with speed: about 0.5 V while cranking, up to about 45 V at 1500 rpm.
Acquisition
Zero-cross comparator with hysteresis producing one clean digital edge per tooth, into a timer input-capture channel. Every edge is timestamped; at 1500 rpm that is an edge every 667 µs. The missing-tooth gap gives the angular reference.
Used for
Speed governing, injection timing (every injection is scheduled against crank angle from here), overspeed shutdown at 1650 rpm inside the tooth interrupt, and reported over CAN in EEC1, PGN 61444.
Fault detection
A gap in the wrong place, no gap where one belongs, or no tooth for 50 ms. Loss of crank signal on a running engine is a shutdown, not an alarm: the ECU can no longer time injection.

Camshaft position

pins 45 / 46 / 44on ECU
Part
Bosch Hall segment sensor, 0 281 002 667 (owner, 22 Nov 2026): 5 V rated (4.7–18 V), open collector sinking 0–20 mA, saturation ≤ 0.5 V (decisions log §29).
Measures
Which of the two crank revolutions is cylinder 1’s firing stroke. The cam wheel’s segment count is not confirmed, so the ECU does not assume one.
Signal
Hall effect, a clean digital square wave. Bosch: sensor pin 3 to O_V_5VCAS (pin 45), pin 2 to I_F_CAS (pin 46), pin 1 to G_R_CAS (pin 44); the three wires twisted.
Acquisition
10 kΩ pull-up to the sensor rail with 16 kΩ to ground (so the pad never sees 5 V), 22 nF and a 3.3 V clamp, into a timer capture channel.
Used for
Cylinder phase, and only that. The decoder learns the wheel: it notes each revolution’s cam edges (how many, and where), and two whole revolutions that differ teach it which is which. One segment, three plus one, two plus two all work; a wheel whose two revolutions look alike is never phased, because not injecting is safer than injecting 360° out (decisions log §39).
Fault detection
A revolution that matches neither learned pattern drops the phase until the next one agrees; one stray edge costs at most one revolution, repeated misses make it learn again. On a wheel other than one segment, cylinder 1’s firing TDC is checked with a timing light at bring-up.
02

Fuel system

Rail pressure

pins 32 / 35 / 08on ECU
Part
Bosch 0 281 006 425, 1800 bar, RDS4 family (owner, 22 Nov 2026; decisions log §30).
Measures
Common-rail fuel pressure, 0–1800 bar. The setpoint is about 830 bar at no load and follows fuel.
Signal
Ratiometric 0.5–4.5 V = 0–1800 bar. Bosch: sensor pin 3 to O_V_5VRAILPS (pin 32), pin 2 to I_A_RAILPS (pin 35), pin 1 to G_R_RAILPS (pin 08), a dedicated ground. With the engine stopped pin 35 reads about 0.5 V against 08.
Acquisition
10k / 16k divider (×16/26), 22 nF and clamp into the ADC, read ratiometrically against the sensor rail.
Used for
The outer loop of the rail cascade: rail error sets the metering valve’s current target. The same pulse width delivers more fuel at higher pressure, so injected quantity always uses both.
Fault detection
Out of range (FMI 3 / 4), and 157/7 (amber) when the rail stays more than 150 bar and 25 % off its target: the valve can no longer hold it.

Fuel metering unit current

pin 88, sensed on-boardon ECU
Part
Bosch CB18, 0 928 400 820 (owner): a proportional solenoid, normally open, no diode inside. Coil 2.5–5 Ω measured (§25); about 1.8 A closes it (Bosch family data, §27).
Signal
Two pins. Bosch: valve pin 1 to ECU pin 88, valve pin 2 to the main-relay line, the two twisted. The ECU switches the low side; the freewheel diode D501 is on the board.
Acquisition
A 50 mΩ shunt and amplifier on the low side, sampled in step with the 1 kHz PWM.
Used for
Inner loop of the rail cascade. Rail pressure sets a current target; current control sets PWM duty, so coil temperature and battery voltage drop out. The coil’s resistance is learned while running, for the fallback.
Fault detection
Open load judged against the current the duty should give; over-current; a reading that is physically impossible is distrusted within 10 ms and the valve runs on the learned coil (scenario 19, §34). Because the valve is normally open, a dead driver means full flow: the rail limit and 157/7 act, and stopping cuts the injectors.

Injector current, ×3

pins 73 / 07 / 29, sensed on-boardon ECU
Part
Bosch CRI-2-16, 0 445 B76 1X3: a bare two-pin solenoid, no diode, its wires twisted. Coil rated 0.2–2.0 Ω at 20 °C (owner; a tester’s pass band, §38).
Measures
Current through each injector during its firing: 18 A peak from the 100 V boost rail, then a 9–11 A hold from the battery.
Signal
A 5 mΩ shunt per bank (cylinders 1 + 3 on high side 03, cylinder 2 on 05), amplified ×20.
Acquisition
Discrete FETs, no driver IC (memo 12). The MCU’s ADC watchdog fires on each threshold crossing and the stage switches in that interrupt; turn-off returns the coil’s energy to the boost reservoir.
Used for
Peak-and-hold. The peak opens the needle; the lower hold keeps it open without cooking the coil. The hold comes from the battery, so it reaches 10 A only on a coil up to 0.79 Ω (13.5 V) / 0.68 Ω (12 V) at 20 °C.
Fault detection
No peak within 60 µs ends the injection (a dead sense must not hold 100 V on a coil); over 26 A ends it; no injection is longer than 5 ms. A hold the battery cannot reach is counted (hold_short) for the bench.
03

Air path

Boost pressure

pins 11 / 41 / 34on ECU
Part
Bosch DS-S3-TF, 0 281 006 102: pressure and temperature in one sensor (owner, 22 Nov 2026; §33).
Measures
Absolute pressure in the intake manifold, after the turbocharger and aftercooler.
Signal
Ratiometric. Bosch: sensor pin 3 to O_V_5VBPS (pin 11), pin 4 to I_A_BPS (pin 41), pin 1 to G_R_BPS (pin 34) through splice V3, shared with the coolant sensor.
Acquisition
INA592 difference amplifier (gain ½) against the shared return, then the 22 nF clamp into the ADC. The sensor ground SGND_34 is tied to the board ground by R633, 0 Ω.
Used for
Air charge for the smoke limit, and reported over CAN.
Fault detection
Range check (FMI 3 / 4).

Charge-air temperature

pin 79on ECU
Part
The NTC inside the boost sensor, Bosch 0 281 006 102. U2 answered 22 Nov 2026: a bare NTC (§33).
Measures
Intake charge temperature after the aftercooler.
Signal
Bosch: sensor pin 2 to I_A_BTS (pin 79); its return is the boost sensor’s, pin 34.
Acquisition
The same front end as coolant: a 2.49 kΩ 0.1 % pull-up to the sensor rail, then 100k / 160k, 22 nF and clamp into the ADC. The firmware takes the divider’s loading out and reads Bosch’s NTC table in ln R.
Used for
Reported over CAN: SPN 105 in IC1, PGN 65270.
Fault detection
Its own debounced range fault (open reads high, short reads low); while faulted a fixed default is sent.

EGR valve position

pins 15 / 37 / 36on ECU
Part
Padmini EGR valve: a DC motor (H-bridge on 59 / 81) and a potentiometer (owner’s drawing, 23 Nov 2026; §36).
Measures
Actual position of the EGR valve, 0–100 % travel.
Signal
Bosch: pot pin 3 to O_V_5VEGR (pin 15), wiper pin 5 to I_A_EGR (pin 37), pin 4 to G_R_EGR (pin 36) through splice V4, shared with the oil sensor. Motor pins 1 / 2 to 59 / 81, twisted.
Acquisition
A plain 100k / 160k divider with no pull-up, 22 nF and clamp: under 0.3 % of full scale for a 5 kΩ track. The firmware assumes 0.5–4.5 V for 0–100 % until the stops are measured on the bench.
Used for
Closed-loop EGR position control through the H-bridge, and stuck-valve diagnosis.
Fault detection
An open wiper reads 0 V (FMI 4). Stuck open or shut is judged from drive, current and position (amber). A stalled motor (over 5 A for 1.5 s) coasts and retries; the third stall holds it off.

Intake throttle

no ECU pinnot on Bosch’s drawing
Status
Closed 10 Nov 2026 (U8). Bosch’s terminal diagram gives a throttle no pin, so the ECU drives none. The owner’s “throttle” is fuelling to reach and hold 1500 rpm: the speed governor, through the injectors and the metering valve.
04

Engine health

These are the sensors that stop the engine before it destroys itself. They earn their place in an ECU that never optimises anything.

Coolant temperature

pin 33on ECU
Part
Bosch 0 281 002 209, NTC M12-H, 2.5 kΩ at 20 °C (owner, 22 Nov 2026; §32).
Measures
Engine coolant temperature. Normal running is roughly 80–95 °C.
Signal
NTC thermistor, two wires. Bosch: sensor pin 1 to I_A_CTS (pin 33), pin 2 through splice V3 to pin 34.
Acquisition
A 2.49 kΩ 0.1 % pull-up to the sensor rail, then 100k / 160k, 22 nF and clamp into the ADC. Converted with Bosch’s own resistance table, interpolated in ln R (a beta law read 2.8 °C high at 100 °C).
Used for
Engine protection by the ECU: amber warning at 100 °C (back at 98), red shutdown with Protect at 104 °C (back at 102). The 25 kVA set’s DSE has its own coolant alarms at 150 / 200 °C, so it never acts and relies on the ECU (§35). Reported over CAN in ET1, PGN 65262.
Fault detection
Open circuit reads high and short reads low, both outside any physical temperature (FMI 3 / 4). An open circuit must not read as “very cold”.

Coolant level switch

pin 43on ECU
Measures
Coolant level, not temperature (owner, 23 Nov 2026; §35). Bosch names the pin I_S_CLS, coolant switch, to terminal 31.
Signal
A switch to ground; closed = level OK.
Acquisition
The common NPN input buffer, debounced.
Used for
Open for 5 s: SPN 111 FMI 18, amber; the engine keeps running. Sent in EFL/P1 byte 8.
Fault detection
A broken wire reads as low level, the safe direction.

Lubricating oil pressure

pins 39 / 80on ECU
Part
Honeywell PX3AN2BF010BSDAX: 0–10 bar sealed gauge, 0.5–4.5 V, with diagnostic rails at 2.5 % and 97.5 % (owner, 22 Nov 2026; §31).
Measures
Engine oil gallery pressure, 0–10 bar.
Signal
Bosch: sensor B to O_V_5VOPS (pin 39), C to I_A_OPS (pin 80), A (ground) through splice V4 to pin 36. The ground is twisted with the 5 V wire; the “76” beside it on the drawing is a line reference, not an ECU pin.
Acquisition
INA592 difference amplifier (gain ½) against the shared return SGND_36, which is tied to the board ground by R634, 0 Ω.
Used for
Reported over CAN in EFL/P1, PGN 65263; the DSE4522 shuts the set down below 0.99 bar, so low-oil protection is the panel’s. With the engine stopped the ECU cross-checks it: 1–2 bar is an implausible oil reading, over 2 bar with no crank teeth says the speed signal is wrong (both amber, §24).
Fault detection
Range, including the sensor’s own diagnostic rails (FMI 3 / 4), and the stopped-engine cross-check above.
05

Electrical

Battery voltage, ×2

pins 04 / 06on ECU
Measures
The two feeds from the main relay’s contact, V_V_BAT_1R (04: electronics and injectors) and V_V_BAT_2R (06: EGR), each behind its own 10 A fuse. The board’s only supply: there is no permanent battery feed.
Acquisition
120 kΩ / 10 kΩ divider into the ADC, one on each ADC instance so both are sampled together.
Used for
Battery alarms, from the 25 kVA set’s own DSE settings (§35): high above 15.0 V (back below 14.5), low below 9.5 V (back above 10.0), 10 s each, amber. Also the metering valve’s feedforward and the EGR diagnosis window.
Fault detection
The two channels should track; a persistent difference means a fuse or a connection.

Ignition / run request

pin 71on ECU
Measures
Whether the panel is commanding the engine to run.
Signal
I_S_T15, switched battery from a GCU relay whose contact is fed from terminal 30 through 5 A; active high.
Acquisition
The common input buffer, and also the wake: pin 71 turns on a second FET on pin 50, so the main relay closes in hardware and the board powers up (Bosch wiring, §18).
Used for
Run enable, and the after-run. Ignition off: the ECU stops the engine, saves its fault memory, and only then releases the main relay, at most 60 s later.
Fault detection
An open wire and a genuine stop request look the same; that is accepted, since both stop the engine.

The override switch's actual function is a guess. Read its state, log it, report it over CAN — but do not let it change what the engine does until somebody has confirmed what the panel intends by it. A switch whose meaning you have assumed is worse than a switch you ignore.

What the controller now demands from us — confirmed 18 Sep 2026

The fitted DSE4522's own configuration takes oil pressure, coolant temperature, coolant level and engine speed from the ECU over CAN rather than from its own analogue inputs, on source addresses 234 (engine) and 44 (instrumentation). Its analogue inputs A and B are configured Not Used for exactly that reason.

Two consequences for this design. Those four values are no longer optional instrumentation — the controller's protections act on them, at 0.99 bar oil pressure. Its coolant alarms on the 25 kVA set are 150 / 200 °C, which never act, so the ECU protects at 100 / 104 °C. And ECU Data Fail is configured to shut the set down, so our CAN transmission is itself a watchdog held by another device. Engine speed is the uncomfortable one: the controller's overspeed trip reads the number we send, which is why handbook §04 now calls a standalone trip module mandatory.

Panel-side sensors

These exist in the machine and appear in the wiring diagram, but they do not reach the ECU. They belong to the panel controller — a Deep Sea DSE4522 MKII AMF, identified 18 Sep 2026; earlier revisions of this page said KG640C, which is what the OEM wiring diagram shows but not what is fitted. What we would add

The OEM sensor set is the minimum to run the engine and meet emissions. These are the additions worth designing space for now, since a connector position or an unused ADC channel costs almost nothing at design time and cannot be retrofitted cheaply.

What future-proofing actually means here

Not fitting every sensor on the list. It means reserving the channels and connector positions so that adding one later is a firmware change and a harness leg, not a board respin. Concretely: leave four ADC channels unrouted but broken out, keep two spare positions on the sensor connector, put the I²C bus on a header even if only the barometric sensor uses it now, and leave the second CAN channel populated.

The MCU has room for all of it — the channel budget below shows the design using well under half of what the part provides. Spending some of that headroom on unpopulated options is the cheapest decision available at this stage.
